Getting Started

1

Go to the Branding tab and enter your organization name, colors, and fonts. Save a profile so your settings persist.

2

Go to the Templates tab and select a layout. This will replace your current document with the template.

3

Replace the placeholder text and images with your content. Use Word's Insert > Pictures to add images.

4

Use the Layout tab to fine-tune page setup, columns, margins, or insert elements like pull quotes and sidebars.

5

Print or save as PDF. See the folding guides below for brochure assembly.

Tri-Fold Brochure: How to Fold

Step 1: Print the document on standard letter paper (8.5 x 11 in.), landscape orientation.

Step 2: Lay the printed sheet face up on a flat surface.

Step 3: Fold the right panel (Panel 3) inward so it covers Panel 2. This is the inside flap.

Step 4: Fold the left panel (Panel 1) over the top, creating the front cover.

Tip: Use a bone folder or the edge of a ruler for crisp folds. For best results, use 80 lb. cardstock or glossy brochure paper.

Bi-Fold Brochure: How to Fold

Step 1: Print the document on letter paper, landscape orientation.

Step 2: Fold the sheet in half so the right panel meets the left panel.

Step 3: The left panel becomes the front cover; the right becomes the back.

Tip: To create a booklet (multiple pages), print double-sided and staple along the fold. Use Word's "Book fold" setting under Layout > Margins > Multiple pages for automatic booklet imposition.

Printing Tips

Paper weight: Standard copy paper (20 lb.) works for drafts. For final prints, use 60-80 lb. cardstock for brochures, or glossy/matte brochure paper for a professional finish.

Margins: Most printers cannot print to the very edge of the paper. If your design has a full-bleed background, you may need to trim after printing or use a print shop.

Double-sided printing: For bi-fold brochures and newsletters, enable duplex (two-sided) printing in your printer settings. Run a test page first to confirm front/back alignment.

Save as PDF: Before sending to a print shop, save your document as PDF (File > Save As > PDF). This preserves formatting across different computers and printers.

Color calibration: Screen colors may differ from printed colors. If color accuracy matters, request a proof from your printer before running a full batch.

About Layout Toolkit
